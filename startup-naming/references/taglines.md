# Taglines

Optional. Use once the user has picked a name, or when they ask. Give **three directions per final pick**, one line each, then a one-line note on where each works best.

The name holds the image; the tagline supplies what the name leaves out on purpose. Together they should say more than either says alone.

## The three directions

1. **Plain line: what it is, for whom.** The clarity line for a homepage header or a pitch deck. Category + audience, or the one outcome the customer gets.
   *Tenfold Arc — Operations software for growing manufacturers.*
2. **Meaning line: unpacks the name.** Finishes the thought the name starts, without repeating its words.
   *Tidecarved — Shaped by what kept coming back.*
3. **Belief line: the founding principle.** Carries the philosophy from intake question 4. Good for an about page, a letterhead, a first hire's offer letter.
   *Founding Order — Build the thing that outlasts the builders.*

(Examples show the form only; write fresh lines for the actual company.)

## Rules for every line

- **8 words or fewer.** If it needs a comma and a second clause, it is a description, not a tagline.
- **Swap test.** Put a competitor's name in front of it. If it still works, it says nothing about this company; rewrite.
- **Don't echo the name.** A tagline that restates the name's word or image wastes the line.
- **No stacked buzzwords.** "AI-powered", "next-generation", "solutions", "empowering", "innovative": one concrete claim beats three of these.
- **Religion and pronunciation passes apply** to the tagline too. No sacred phrasing ("let there be…", "born again", "the way, the truth…").
- **Built to last.** A tagline should hold for years; a campaign slogan is a different job.

## The glance test

Read name + tagline as if passing it on a hoarding at speed: about three seconds. The reader should get what the company does, or what it stands for, and keep the name.

## Output

```
**NAME**
1. Plain — "…"  → homepage, deck
2. Meaning — "…"  → brand story, social bio
3. Belief — "…"  → about page, letterhead
```

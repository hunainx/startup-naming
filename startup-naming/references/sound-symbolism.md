# Sound symbolism

Speech sounds carry a feeling before the meaning lands. Use this to steer candidates toward the feeling the user picked in intake question 3, and to break ties. It never outranks meaning: a name with a strong meaning and slightly off sounds beats a name that sounds right and means nothing.

These associations are strongest for English listeners. Check them against the markets the company will sell in.

## Consonants

| Sounds | What they signal | Examples |
|--------|------------------|----------|
| **Hard stops**: k, t, p, b, d, g | Strength, precision, decisiveness, weight | Keel, Anvil, Tenfold, Ballast |
| **Hisses and breaths**: s, f, sh, v, z, th | Motion, speed, smoothness, continuity | Swell, Further, Signal, Spindrift |
| **Hums**: m, n | Warmth, calm, trust, approachability | Morrow, Measure, Canon |
| **Flowing**: l, r, w | Ease and openness (l, w); energy and grit (r) | Loam, Windward, Ridge, Grit |

Clusters carry extra colour: *gr-* feels like grip and ground (Grain, Groundswell), *st-/str-* like firmness and tension (Still, Standard), *fl-* like lightness and movement.

## Vowels

- **Front vowels** (the sounds in *see, sit, say, set*): small, quick, light, precise.
- **Back vowels** (the sounds in *moon, stone, law, arc*): large, deep, slow, weighty.

The well-known "bouba/kiki" finding, where people across many languages match a rounded shape to *bouba* and a spiky one to *kiki*, is the same effect: rounded back vowels feel big and soft; front vowels with hard stops feel small and sharp.

## Endings

- **Hard stop** (Arc, Keel, Kept): decisive, closed, complete.
- **Nasal** (Canon, Season, Horizon): lingering, warm.
- **Open vowel**: friendly, expansive, consumer-leaning.
- **Participle -ed / -n** (Tidecarved, Foamborn): a finished action; the name reads as something that has already been through the test.

## Matching to the intake answers

| Feeling (intake Q3) | Lean toward | Go easy on |
|---------------------|-------------|------------|
| Established institution | Hard stops, back vowels, closed endings | Bouncy rhythms, open vowel endings |
| Deep philosophical | Back vowels, nasals, participle compounds | Clipped one-syllable punch |
| Inspiring / uplifting | Fricatives and liquids, rising two-beat rhythm | Heavy clusters |
| Modern minimal | One or two syllables, a single hard stop | Long compounds, soft trailing endings |
| Warm / human | m, n, l, open vowels | Stacked hard stops (k-t-k) |
| Playful | Repeated sounds, open vowels, light front vowels | Heavy back-vowel weight |

## Quick check on a shortlist

1. Say it aloud five times at normal speed. It should not trip.
2. Put it in a sentence: "I work at ___." "___ just raised its Series A." The rhythm should carry.
3. Compare its dominant sounds with the competitor scan's "Sound gap" line. Standing apart from the cluster is worth a point on a tie.

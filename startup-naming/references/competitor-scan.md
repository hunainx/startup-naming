# Competitor scan

Do this before generating a single candidate. A name is judged next to its neighbours, not on its own. The scan tells you which shapes are already crowded, which words are already spent, and where a new name can stand apart.

## 1. Build the list (10–20 names)

Use WebSearch. Aim for a spread:

- **Direct:** same offer, same buyer. 5–10 names.
- **Adjacent:** a neighbouring category the buyer also pays for. 3–5 names.
- **Leaders:** the 2–3 names that set the category's expectations.
- **Aspirational:** companies the user wants to be mentioned beside (ask if they gave none in intake).

Fewer than 10 means the pattern counts below are guesswork; keep searching.

## 2. Record each name

One row per competitor:

| Name | Domain they actually use | Shape | Sound | Letters | Metaphor world |
|------|--------------------------|-------|-------|---------|----------------|

- **Domain they actually use:** exact .com, or a compromise (getX.com, Xhq.com, X.io, X.ai). Count the compromises; they show how much of the category's vocabulary is already spent at the registry. That count is evidence for going straight to compounds and two-word names.
- **Shape:** single real word · real-word compound · two words · coined · descriptive (says what it does) · founder or surname · acronym · Latin/Greek root.
- **Sound:** hard-led (k, t, p, b, g) or soft-led (m, n, l, s, f, v); short and clipped or long and flowing. See `sound-symbolism.md` if needed.
- **Metaphor world:** the territory the name borrows from (sky, speed, geometry, nature, myth, none).

## 3. Find the clusters

Group by shape, then by metaphor world. For each group count the members:

- **0:** open. Check it isn't empty for a reason (e.g. no one uses playful names in trust-critical categories).
- **1–2:** lightly used; still viable.
- **3–5:** crowded; a new name here needs to be clearly better than its neighbours.
- **Most of the list:** the category convention. Joining it makes a name invisible; breaking it makes a name stand out but must still feel credible to the buyer.

Also note the clichés: prefixes, suffixes and roots that three or more competitors share (Nova-, -ify, Flow, Sync, Pay, -ly, Apex, Nexus…).

## 4. Write the scan brief

Five lines, placed in working notes under the intake answers:

```
Crowded:     <shapes/worlds with 3+ members, with counts>
Open:        <shapes/worlds with 0–1 members that still suit the buyer>
Sound gap:   <e.g. "all soft and vowel-ending; hard-led names stand out">
Spent words: <competitor words and shared roots, now added to exclusions>
Domain note: <n of N competitors on a compromised domain>
```

Feed "Open" and "Sound gap" into the territory choice in step 3. "Spent words" are exclusions for the whole session. Show the user the brief in two or three lines only if it changes direction; otherwise keep it internal and move on.
